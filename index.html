Case studies

Customer.io · Amplitude · lifecycle email

Targeted member campaigns from product behavior

Marketing needed broadcast and campaign management that was not a one-off export. I designed the Customer.io integration so member and claims attributes, plus event schemas, landed in a shape the team could use for automated emails and lifecycle campaigns — without waiting on a new pipeline for every send.

Amplitude events from the website and the app fed the same Customer.io path. Product behavior — who signed in, who stalled in a flow, who completed a key action — sat next to membership data, so outreach could be targeted instead of sprayed to the whole book.

The result was less time assembling lists by hand, and a clearer read on how members actually engaged — opens, reads, and campaign performance — instead of guessing after the send.

PySpark · Databricks · Delta Lake · Looker · pytest

Outbound framework with observability and data-quality gates

Partner delivery used to mean a new outbound pipeline for every destination. I led a PySpark framework on Databricks Delta Lake so a new feed was configuration and reuse, not a three-day build. The same path now powers outbound delivery, and runtime dropped about 80%.

Looker sat on top of operational metrics as the observability layer: which files went out, volume, latency, and failures. Ops, engineering, and leadership could inspect the same KPIs instead of digging through job logs after a miss.

Underneath, I tightened pytest coverage on shared functions and added rigorous cases for the paths that actually break feeds. A modular validation layer ran in front of delivery — schema, completeness, and data-quality checks — so bad files failed in the platform, not at the partner.

Oracle · Python · Shell · SFTP · Tableau

Inbound framework: SFTP sync to analytics-ready data

Upstream files arrived on SFTP on their own clocks, in their own layouts. I architected an inbound framework in Python and shell scripting that discovered, pulled, and synced those drops — landing raw files, tracking what had already been loaded, and retrying the rest without manual copies. Oracle held the structured store the loads landed in and served from.

Once in, the same path prepared data for Tableau: typed tables, consistent keys, and downstream-friendly grain so analysts were not cleaning vendor files in the workbook. Inbound and outbound shared the same reliability ideas — idempotent loads, visible failures, and checks before anything was marked ready.
